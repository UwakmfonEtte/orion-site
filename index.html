Two floors, and only one of them depends on opinion.

Every Orion sits above two numbers. The market floor is whatever the cheapest seller will accept today. The backing floor is the treasury divided by circulating supply — an on-chain figure that owes nothing to sentiment. You can always exchange one for the other: burn your Orion and the treasury pays out its share.

What redemption does to the rest

Every redemption pays out backing less a 5% fee. The fee stays, the supply falls — so the Orions that remain are each worth more than before. Move the slider and watch it happen.

Why the floor holds

If the market floor ever falls materially below the backing floor, anyone can buy an Orion, redeem it, and keep the difference. That trade is available to everybody, so it does not survive being noticed — buying pressure closes the gap.

Nothing in the protocol fixes a price. The tether is simply that selling below backing means handing a stranger free money.

Where the treasury grows

  • 100% of mint proceeds
  • 50% of secondary royaltiesroyalty is 10% total
  • 5% redemption fee
  • 5% lending interest
  • Rental fees
  • Treasury-stock profits

Redemption charges 5%, which stays in the treasury. Because supply falls while those fees remain, redemptions raise the backing of every Orion that stays.

Borrow

Against your backing

Borrow against 100% of your Orion's backing value at a fixed 5%, over a choice of terms. A hard redeemable value makes unusually clean collateral — there is no oracle to argue with.

Rent

The protocol rents, not you

The treasury buys Orions with 10% of secondary royalties and rents those out at 10% of backing — yield on assets the protocol already owns. You never lend your own token.

Redeem

Exit at backing

Burn the token, take its share of treasury in ETH, less the 5% fee. Supply falls and backing per remaining Orion rises. The door stays open — that is what makes the floor mean anything.
